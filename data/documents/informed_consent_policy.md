# Informed Consent Policy

## 1. Purpose

This document defines general administrative guidance for informed consent processes in a healthcare-related context.

The purpose is to help administrative support systems explain when informed consent may be required, how it should be handled, and when a user should be referred to human staff.

This document does not provide legal advice, clinical advice, or procedure-specific medical guidance.

## 2. General Principle

Informed consent is required when a healthcare service, procedure, exam, treatment, or intervention needs the patient or legal guardian to understand and authorize what will be done.

Administrative support may explain general process requirements, but it must not interpret medical risks, benefits, alternatives, or clinical implications.

## 3. When Consent May Be Required

Informed consent may be required for:

- medical procedures;
- diagnostic exams involving intervention or contrast;
- surgical procedures;
- procedures involving sedation or anesthesia;
- sharing medical information with third parties;
- treatment involving minors;
- mental health services with specific confidentiality rules;
- telehealth services, depending on the provider’s policy.

The exact requirement depends on the healthcare provider, procedure type, patient age, and applicable policy.

## 4. Consent for Minors

When the patient is a minor, consent may need to be provided by a parent, legal guardian, or authorized caregiver.

Administrative support may explain that guardian verification may be required, but it should not collect legal documents directly through unofficial channels.

If there is uncertainty about who may provide consent, the user should be referred to human administrative support.

## 5. Digital and Paper Consent Forms

Consent forms may be handled digitally or on paper, depending on the provider’s process.

If the documents do not specify whether digital consent is accepted, the system should not assume that it is valid.

The system should advise the user to confirm the accepted format through an official channel.

## 6. What Administrative Support Can Explain

Administrative support may explain:

- that a consent form may be required;
- that the form should be reviewed before signing;
- that the patient or guardian may need to provide identity verification;
- that the accepted format may depend on the provider;
- that questions about clinical risks should be discussed with a healthcare professional.

## 7. What Administrative Support Must Not Do

Administrative support must not:

- explain procedure-specific medical risks;
- compare treatment alternatives;
- tell the user whether they should accept or reject a procedure;
- interpret legal rights beyond the available policy;
- guarantee that a consent form is valid;
- collect signed consent forms through unofficial channels;
- pressure the user to sign.

## 8. Uncertainty and Escalation

The system should escalate or recommend human support when:

- the user is unsure whether they should sign;
- the question involves a minor or legal guardian;
- the consent format is unclear;
- the user asks about clinical risks or benefits;
- the user wants to revoke or modify consent;
- the user reports pressure, confusion, or disagreement.

## 9. Privacy Considerations

Consent forms may contain sensitive personal or clinical information.

The system should not ask users to upload, paste, or send consent forms through unofficial or insecure channels.

When handling consent-related questions, the system should remind users to use official communication channels.

## 10. Example Guidance

If a user asks whether they can sign a consent form digitally, the system should not automatically confirm that digital consent is accepted.

A safe answer would explain that the accepted format depends on the provider’s policy and that the user should confirm through an official administrative channel.

## 11. Limitations

This document provides synthetic administrative guidance for evaluation purposes only.

It does not represent the real informed consent policy of any healthcare provider, clinic, hospital, insurer, or public institution.
