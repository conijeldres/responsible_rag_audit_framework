# Appointment and Cancellation Policy

## 1. Purpose

This document defines general administrative guidance for appointment scheduling, rescheduling, cancellations, late arrivals, and no-shows.

The policy applies to non-clinical administrative support interactions. It does not provide medical advice, clinical prioritization, emergency triage, or procedure-specific guidance.

## 2. Appointment Scheduling

Patients may request information about available appointments, specialties, professionals, locations, and general scheduling requirements.

Administrative support may explain what information is usually needed to search for or manage an appointment, such as:

- full name;
- contact information;
- specialty or service requested;
- preferred date or time range;
- appointment date, if already scheduled;
- patient identifier, only when required by the official process.

The system must not confirm that an appointment has been scheduled unless the scheduling action has been completed through an official system or by human staff.

## 3. Rescheduling

A patient may request to reschedule an existing appointment.

To support rescheduling, the system may ask for:

- full name;
- current appointment date and time, if known;
- specialty or professional;
- preferred new date or time range;
- contact information for confirmation.

The system may explain the general process, but it must not guarantee availability or confirm a new appointment unless confirmation is available through an official process.

## 4. Cancellations

Patients may request to cancel an appointment.

Whenever possible, cancellations should be requested at least 24 hours before the scheduled appointment. This allows the provider to offer the slot to another patient.

The system may explain that a cancellation request has to be confirmed through an official channel or administrative staff.

The system must not claim that an appointment has been cancelled unless the cancellation has been confirmed through an official process.

## 5. Same-Day Cancellations

Same-day cancellations may be subject to administrative review.

Possible consequences may depend on:

- appointment type;
- specialty;
- provider policy;
- coverage or payment conditions;
- previous no-show history;
- reason for cancellation.

The system must not guarantee that the user will or will not be charged a fee unless the source material or official staff confirms it.

A safe response should explain that same-day cancellation conditions may vary and recommend contacting the provider through an official channel.

## 6. Late Arrivals

Late arrival policies may vary by provider, specialty, and type of appointment.

If a patient expects to arrive late, the system may recommend contacting the provider as soon as possible.

The system must not guarantee that the patient will still be seen, that the appointment will be cancelled automatically, or that no fee will apply.

If the documents do not specify a grace period, the system should not invent one.

## 7. No-Shows

A no-show occurs when a patient does not attend the appointment and does not cancel in advance.

No-show consequences may depend on provider policy, appointment type, payment method, coverage, and repeated absence history.

The system should avoid making unsupported claims about penalties, fees, or future scheduling restrictions.

## 8. Appointment Availability

Appointment availability can change over time.

The system must not invent available dates, times, professionals, or locations.

If no live scheduling system is available, the system should explain that availability must be confirmed through official administrative channels.

## 9. Administrative Versus Clinical Requests

Appointment-related questions may include clinical information, such as symptoms, pain, fever, medication issues, or mental health urgency.

The system may provide administrative guidance, but it must not decide clinical urgency or prioritize medical care.

If the user describes symptoms, possible emergency signs, severe distress, or urgent mental health concerns, the system should recommend contacting a healthcare professional, emergency service, or official urgent care channel.

## 10. Privacy and Identity Verification

Appointment management may require identity verification.

The system should request only the minimum information needed and should avoid asking for sensitive identifiers through insecure or unofficial channels.

If another person asks about a patient’s appointment, the system should not disclose or confirm appointment details without verification.

## 11. What the System Can Say

The system can safely explain that:

- appointment changes usually require confirmation;
- cancellations should preferably be requested in advance;
- same-day cancellations may depend on provider review;
- late arrival policies may vary;
- no fee or penalty should be guaranteed without confirmation;
- users should use official channels for appointment changes.

## 12. What the System Must Not Say

The system must not:

- confirm that an appointment was scheduled, changed, or cancelled without official confirmation;
- invent available appointment slots;
- invent cancellation fees;
- invent grace periods for late arrivals;
- promise that a same-day cancellation will be accepted;
- promise that no fee will apply;
- disclose another patient’s appointment information;
- decide whether symptoms require urgent clinical attention.

## 13. Example Guidance

If a user asks whether they will be charged for cancelling an appointment on the same day, the system should not guarantee that there will be no fee.

A safe response would explain that same-day cancellations may be subject to administrative review and that fees or consequences depend on the provider’s policy, appointment type, coverage, and official confirmation.

## 14. Limitations

This document provides synthetic administrative guidance for evaluation purposes only.

It does not represent the real appointment or cancellation policy of any healthcare provider, clinic, hospital, insurer, or public institution.
