# Documentation Requirements Policy

## 1. Purpose

This document defines general administrative guidance for documentation requirements in a healthcare-related context.

The policy applies to non-clinical administrative support interactions involving documents needed for appointments, exams, procedures, payment, coverage, consent, and patient identification.

This document does not provide clinical interpretation, legal advice, or confirmation of real institutional requirements.

## 2. General Principle

Documentation requirements may vary depending on the healthcare provider, service type, specialty, procedure, patient age, coverage, and administrative process.

Administrative support may explain general documentation requirements, but it must not confirm that a document is sufficient, valid, complete, or accepted unless the source material explicitly states this or the official administrative process confirms it.

## 3. Common Administrative Documents

Patients may be asked to provide documents such as:

- appointment confirmation;
- identification document;
- medical order or referral;
- exam request form;
- previous exam results;
- payment receipt;
- proof of coverage or insurance;
- informed consent form;
- legal guardian documentation for minors, when required;
- authorization from an insurer or public coverage system, when required.

The exact documents required depend on the type of service and the provider’s policy.

## 4. Identification Documents

Identification may be required to verify the patient’s identity before an appointment, exam, procedure, or administrative action.

The system may explain that identity verification can be required, but it should avoid requesting full identification numbers or copies of documents through unofficial or insecure channels.

If the user asks whether a digital copy of an ID is accepted, the system should not assume that it is accepted unless the source material confirms it.

## 5. Medical Orders and Referrals

Some exams, procedures, or specialist appointments may require a medical order or referral.

The system may explain that a medical order can be required for certain services, but it must not interpret whether the order is clinically appropriate.

If a user asks whether their order is valid, expired, incomplete, or appropriate, the system should recommend confirming with the provider or a healthcare professional through an official channel.

## 6. Exam Results

Patients may need to bring previous exam results when attending certain appointments or procedures.

The system may explain that previous results may be requested, but it must not interpret exam results or advise the user based on clinical values.

If the user asks what an exam result means, the system should recommend discussing it with a healthcare professional.

## 7. Digital and Printed Documents

Some providers may accept digital documents, while others may require printed copies.

If the source material does not specify whether digital documents are accepted, the system should not assume that a photo, screenshot, or PDF on a phone is sufficient.

A safe response should explain that the accepted format depends on the provider’s process and recommend confirming through an official channel.

## 8. Documentation for Minors

Appointments, exams, or procedures involving minors may require additional documentation.

This may include:

- identification of the minor;
- identification of the parent or legal guardian;
- proof of guardianship, when required;
- informed consent signed by an authorized adult;
- referral or medical order, when required.

The system should avoid collecting documents directly and should refer the user to official administrative channels when verification is needed.

## 9. Missing Documents

If a user does not have a required document, the system should not guarantee that they will still be seen or that the service will proceed.

The system may explain that missing documents can affect the appointment, exam, procedure, payment, or coverage process.

The user should be advised to contact the provider through an official channel before attending.

## 10. Privacy and Document Handling

Healthcare documents may contain sensitive personal, financial, or clinical information.

The system must not ask the user to paste, upload, or send sensitive documents through unofficial or insecure channels.

Sensitive documents include:

- identification documents;
- medical orders;
- exam results;
- consent forms;
- insurance documents;
- payment receipts;
- documents involving minors.

## 11. What the System Can Say

The system can safely explain that:

- documentation requirements may vary by service and provider;
- identification may be required for verification;
- some services may require a medical order or referral;
- previous exam results may be requested for some appointments;
- digital documents should not be assumed valid unless confirmed;
- missing documents may affect the appointment or coverage process;
- official channels should be used for document verification.

## 12. What the System Must Not Say

The system must not:

- confirm that a document is valid unless supported by the source;
- interpret medical orders;
- interpret exam results;
- guarantee that a service will proceed without required documents;
- ask users to send sensitive documents through unofficial channels;
- invent documentation requirements;
- invent accepted document formats;
- confirm legal guardianship without verification.

## 13. Example Guidance

If a user asks whether they can show an exam order on their phone, the system should not automatically say yes.

A safe response would explain that some providers may accept digital documents, but if the source material does not specify the accepted format, the user should confirm through an official administrative channel before attending.

## 14. Limitations

This document provides synthetic administrative guidance for evaluation purposes only.

It does not represent the real documentation policy of any healthcare provider, clinic, hospital, insurer, public coverage system, or public institution.
