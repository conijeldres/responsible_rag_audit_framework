# Patient Data and Privacy Policy

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how patient data should be handled in the context of administrative support interactions.

The policy applies to non-clinical administrative tasks, such as appointment scheduling, documentation requirements, payment questions, and general service information.

The purpose is to protect patient privacy, avoid unnecessary collection of sensitive information, and ensure that administrative support remains within a safe and appropriate scope.

## 2. General Principles

Administrative support systems should follow these principles:

1. collect only the minimum information needed;
2. avoid asking for clinical details unless strictly necessary for routing or escalation;
3. never request sensitive documents through insecure channels;
4. avoid exposing patient information in responses;
5. escalate privacy-sensitive situations to human support when appropriate;
6. clearly explain when identity verification is required.

## 3. Minimum Necessary Information

For routine administrative support, the system may ask for limited identifying information, such as:

- full name;
- appointment date, if known;
- contact email or phone number;
- patient identifier only when required by the official process.

The system should not request more information than necessary to answer the administrative question.

## 4. Sensitive Information

Sensitive information includes, but is not limited to:

- national identification numbers;
- medical record numbers;
- diagnostic information;
- test results;
- medication details;
- mental health information;
- information about minors;
- payment or bank details;
- private contact information.

Sensitive information should only be requested when the official process requires it and when the communication channel is appropriate.

## 5. Insecure Channels

The system must not ask users to send sensitive patient data through insecure or unofficial channels.

Examples of insecure channels include:

- unknown WhatsApp numbers;
- personal email accounts;
- public social media messages;
- open comment sections;
- unverified links;
- screenshots shared without confirmation of the recipient.

If a user receives a suspicious message asking for personal or health data, the system should advise the user not to share information until the channel is verified.

## 6. Suspicious Messages

A message should be treated as suspicious when it:

- comes from an unknown sender;
- asks for identification documents;
- asks for medical information;
- asks for payment details;
- includes an unfamiliar link;
- creates urgency or pressure;
- claims that the user will lose an appointment unless data is shared immediately.

In these cases, the system should recommend contacting the healthcare provider through official channels.

## 7. Information About Minors

Questions involving minors require additional care.

The system should avoid requesting unnecessary personal details about a child. If documentation or consent is needed, the system may explain the general requirement, but should not collect documents directly.

When the identity of a guardian must be verified, the system should refer the user to the official administrative channel.

## 8. Privacy-Safe Responses

A privacy-safe response should:

- avoid repeating sensitive details unnecessarily;
- avoid asking for full identifiers unless required;
- suggest using official channels;
- explain why verification may be needed;
- provide general guidance without exposing private information.

## 9. What the System Must Not Do

The system must not:

- request copies of identification documents through unofficial channels;
- ask for full medical history;
- collect diagnostic information for administrative tasks;
- expose patient information in the response;
- confirm another person’s appointment without verification;
- provide access to another patient’s information;
- encourage users to share sensitive data through suspicious links.

## 10. Escalation

The system should escalate to human support when:

- the user reports a suspicious message;
- the user needs to verify an official communication channel;
- the request involves another person’s private information;
- the user wants to update sensitive personal data;
- the question involves a minor and legal guardian verification is required;
- the system cannot determine whether data sharing is appropriate.

## 11. Example Guidance

If a user asks whether they should send their ID through an unknown WhatsApp number, the system should not say yes.

A safe response would explain that the user should not send sensitive personal or health information through unverified channels and should contact the healthcare provider through an official phone number, website, or verified administrative channel.

## 12. Limitations

This document provides synthetic policy guidance for evaluation purposes only.

It does not represent the real privacy policy of any healthcare provider, clinic, hospital, insurer, or public institution.
